Adoption process

How to adopt from GPRS

Thank you for wanting to adopt a Great Pyrenees rescue. When you adopt a Pyr, you're adding a valuable member to your family. We'd love to get to know you before moving forward.

  1. Apply online

    Four short sections: about you, your home, yard and vet, your pets, and the dog you love. A volunteer contacts you within 3–4 days.

  2. Let's talk

    A screener reviews your application, talks with you about possible matches, and lets you know if you're approved.

  3. Sign the contract

    Once approved, you complete the adoption contract. We email an invoice for the adoption fee. Pay by card, PayPal, Zelle or Venmo.

  4. Bring your Pyr home

    Local pickup, or we arrange transport with an experienced pet transport provider. You can also arrange your own.

Before you apply

What to expect

  • Fees: $300–$450 depending on the dog (bonded pairs are listed together). The exact fee is on each dog's profile.
  • Fencing: We require secure, visible fencing. Most of our dogs need a 6-foot fence. Pyrs naturally roam.
  • Renters: We'll ask for your landlord's contact details and approval for a large-breed dog.
  • Vet reference: Have your veterinarian's name and phone number ready.
  • Vetting included: Vaccinations, including rabies, plus spay or neuter.
  • Payment: Credit card or PayPal through the invoice link. Zelle and Venmo also accepted. Ask your screener.

After you submit

Click "Complete the Form" at the bottom of the application. You'll land on your My Rescue page, where you can review and print your application, and you'll receive a confirmation email.

A volunteer will contact you within 3 to 4 days. Please check your spam or junk folder in case our email gets misdirected.

Please don't fill out the adoption contract until a screener tells you to. When you're approved, use the adoption contract.

FAQ

Common questions

I have certain preferences. Can you help me find the right dog?
Yes. While every dog is adoptable, every dog is different, with their own personality and preferences. That's why we match certain dogs with certain owners. Browse our available dogs for details on each one, and tell your screener what you're looking for.
Do you have puppies or younger dogs?
We have both puppies and teens. Puppies haven't fully developed their personalities yet. Just like children, they grow and change. A teen dog gives you the playfulness of a puppy with a better sense of who they are.
Why do Pyrenees puppies need an adult dog in the home?
Great Pyrenees learn their role from other dogs, not from people. Older dogs teach appropriate dog interaction and socialization, including that biting hurts. Without proper socialization as puppies, they can become very difficult teens and adults.
Can I foster to adopt?
Yes. As a foster, the dog stays with you until someone adopts them. If you decide you want to keep your foster dog, contact us as soon as possible so you can apply before anyone else is approved. Learn about fostering.
I don't live in Texas. Can I still adopt?
Yes. We transport and adopt dogs to Washington, Oregon, Idaho, California, New Mexico and Arizona, along with Texas. We're happy to arrange transport through one of our experienced pet transport providers, and many of our dogs have already traveled to the Pacific Northwest. You're also welcome to arrange your own transport.
Can I adopt a livestock guardian dog from you?
No. Our Pyrenees and Pyr mixes were surrendered because they weren't good with livestock, or were strays found wandering away from their livestock, so we can't safely place them in livestock homes. Our dogs live in foster homes where they learn to be house dogs. For a working dog, we highly recommend Bluebonnet Animal Rescue, a Texas rescue that specializes in livestock guardian dogs for hobby farms and small livestock homes.
